to prove f(W_1, W_2) cannot exist for multiplication width,  
find a counterexample with 2 pairs of intervals that have the same input width,  
but different product widths  
therefore f(W_1, W_2) cannot be a function

```
(define i1 (make-interval 3 4))
(define i2 (make-interval -1 2))
(define i3 (make-interval 5 6))
(width i1) > 1/2
(width i3) > 1/2
(width i2) > 1 1/2

(mul-interval i1 i2) > (-4 . 8) > width = (8 - -4) / 2 = 6
(mul-interval i3 i2) > (-6 . 12) > width = (12 - -6) / 2 = 9
```
width of i1 and i3 are equal, yet multiplication with i2 results in unequal intervals with different widths  
therefore f(W_1, W_2) for the width of multiplication of intervals can't be a function.
